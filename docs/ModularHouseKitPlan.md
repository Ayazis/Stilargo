# Modular House Kit — Plan

Goal: buildings that match the concept art (`docs/ConceptArt/House_1.png`) and don't look like toys, built from
reusable pieces so the whole city can share one style.

## Why a kit

The first House 1 attempt (`Tools/Art/build_house_1.py`, on `feature/game-feel-city`) places every stone, sill and
shutter as a scripted box. Scanned textures, bevels and weathering helped, but it still reads as "Lego": every piece is
a sharp, perfectly regular block, and each house is unique, so nothing is reused.

We studied a professional free kit, Poly Haven's CC0 `modular_urban_apartments_facade`, as a reference:

| Their kit | Our first attempt |
|---|---|
| ~80 modules on a 3 m grid: wall panels (plain, window, door, corner, angled) plus horizontal bands (base, dado, cornice, crown) and piers | One script places every stone for one house |
| Walls: flat panels, 4 to ~600 faces each, with a tiling texture | Now similar (scanned brick) |
| Mouldings and stone: **trim sheets**. A cornice is 9–36 faces; carving and wear are baked into a strip texture from a detailed sculpt | Bare boxes with a generic texture (**main cause of the toy look**) |
| Windows and doors: a few detailed hero pieces (~400–1,200 faces) sharing one texture atlas, reused everywhere | Thin boxes, unique per house |
| Detail mostly **baked into textures** | Detail mostly geometry |

So detail comes from textures baked from detailed models, and pieces are modules that get reused.

## Principles

- **Bake, don't box.** Detailed geometry (sculpted, eroded, chipped) lives only in Blender source files. In-game
  pieces are simple shapes that carry the detail in baked normal, AO, albedo and roughness maps.
- **Shared texture sets.** One stone trim sheet, one hero atlas (windows, doors, shutters, lanterns, flower boxes), and
  tiling materials (brick, plaster, roof tiles). Every building reuses them.
- **Grid.** Bays are 2.4 m wide (to be confirmed against House 1's proportions). The ground storey is 3.4 m and upper
  storeys are 3.1 m. Pieces snap to the grid so they can be combined freely.
- **Scripted and reproducible.** Every piece, bake and texture is generated by a script under `Tools/Art/`, run
  headless (`blender --background --factory-startup --python ...`), the same way `build_corner_house.py` and
  `build_house_1.py` work.
- **HDRP materials only through `HdrpMaterials`.** Each texture set gets a `SurfaceTextures` folder under
  `Assets/_Project/Art/Textures/` (`_albedo.jpg`, `_normal.jpg` OpenGL, `_mask.png` HDRP mask map).
- **Only CC0 third-party sources** (Poly Haven, ambientCG), recorded in `Assets/_Project/Art/Textures/CREDITS.md`.

## Steps

Each step ends with a Windows build and a visual check in the exe by the user.

### 1. Stone trim sheet (proof of concept)
- In Blender, script detailed source models: quoin stones (long and short), window-surround jamb blocks, arch voussoirs
  plus keystone, sill with drip edge, plinth blocks, the eaves moulding profile, and the door arch.
  Erosion and chipping come from displacement and noise; edges are rounded and corners chipped.
- Bake them onto one strip-layout texture (about 2048×2048): albedo (cream sandstone with grime and moss low down),
  normal, AO and roughness, packed into an HDRP mask map.
- Low-detail in-game pieces are UV-mapped onto the strips.
- **Check:** swap House 1's stone boxes for these pieces. Stone should read as carved and weathered, not toy blocks.
  If not, revisit before going further.

### 2. Hero atlas
- Detailed window (casements, glazing bars, putty lines, curtains), louvred shutters (slight warp and peeling paint),
  arched panelled door with knocker, lantern, number plaque and flower box. All share one baked atlas.
- Window variants: ground (taller), upper, and with or without flower box.

### 3. Wall modules
- Per bay: plain, window, door, plus corner pieces with alternating quoins, using flat walls with the scanned
  `red_brick` texture. A plaster variant uses `white_plaster_rough_01`.
- Horizontal bands: plinth, storey band (optional), eaves moulding. All use the trim sheet.

### 4. Roof modules
- Hip and gable roof pieces sized to the bay grid: a clay-tile texture with real tile geometry only at eaves, hips
  and ridge, plus gutters, a chimney and a skylight.

### 5. Assemble House 1 from the kit
- An assembler (editor script) builds House 1 from a small layout description (bays per side, door position,
  flower boxes) into a prefab, replacing `House1Assets`' single FBX.
- The showcase spot keeps using it (`WorldPrefabBuilder` → `_showcaseHousePrefab`).

### 6. Second house from the same kit
- Build a different house (other bay counts, plaster instead of brick, a gable roof) only by changing the layout.
  This proves the kit generalises before we use it city-wide.

## Open questions
- Bay width and storey heights for the whole city: are they fixed, or per district?
- Should layouts live in JSON config (`Assets/StreamingAssets/Config/`) or in ScriptableObjects? They reference
  authored assets, so ScriptableObjects is the likely answer.
- Poly budget per house at the gameplay camera distance (current House 1 is about 156k faces, which is too many for a
  whole city); LODs are needed for city-wide use.
- Who reviews the look between steps (FL / Ci)?
